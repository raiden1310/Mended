IDEA LOCK · Build Sprint

The idea, in one line: AI repair desk for jewelry retail stores
Why me (at least 1 of 3: an audience that trusts me, years inside the workflow, data nobody else has): Audience that trusts me, years inside workflow. I want to eventually build an AI Jewelry Repair Robot and this is a great starting point for data collection.

GOAL
The one goal they hire it for (money, time, status or life): Time
Delta 4 (the steps today → the steps with my product): 12 -> 7
The sin it rides (optional): Sloth

USER
The trigger (when the pain hits): At the time of taking in a repair
Today's path, step by step (including not solving it at all): Customer brings in a piece for repair, sales associate checks it, takes pictures, enters the transaction in POS, may or may not provide estimate, creates the ticket, routes it to a jeweler/vendor, communicates with customer on status updates, customer picks up piece when ready, associate finalizes the sale
Who they trust on this decision: Peers (other store sales associates or people from the industry)
Would they pay? (what exists today that people pay for): Yes (they pay for this in a POS, which is extremely manual)

PRODUCT
Onboarding (how a first-time user feels the value fastest): Implementation needs an integration with a POS to drive value. After that, in 1 step - click pictures of the item for a customer and the rest is taken care of.
The core loop (user stories, written by me): As a sales associate, I want to be able to create a repair ticket for a customer quickly and accurately so that I can go back to selling as soon as possible and don't have to handle discrepancy disputes later.
Coming back (optional): Yes. For every customer walking in for a repair.
The AI-first part (onboarding, engagement or the core loop): Core loop

MARKET
Tailwinds (where funding is going, what Google Trends shows, timing): The jewelry repairs market is growing and my conversations with store owners has revealed a need for this.
Competitors (and the flows I liked, with screenshots and why): TheEdge, Luxare, Jewel360, Luxe - all have a repair intake module and can eventually build this as a feature.
Size and fit (how many people in my extended network fit): 10+

V1 SCOPE (decided at lock, 2 Oct)
How v1 works without a POS integration: Take a picture, Create a condition record, Identify services needed, Estimate the repair price for the customer, Create a repair ticket, Text the customer
The 2 steps left with my product (from today's 6): Check the piece, Estimate the repair, Route the repair, Create the ticket and update the customer – All of these steps disappear.

One remains – Click pictures of the item
One gets added – Review and approve the estimate
Why a jeweler picks this over a feature in their POS: Saves time and allows sales associates to focus on selling, eventually growing the business
My first 3 test stores (not Luxare customers): Helzberg, Lenox, John Atencio
